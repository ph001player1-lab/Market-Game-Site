WHAT IT IS

Your decision. The market's answer.

Several companies sit down around one market and start competing for the same customers. Each turn is a game month. You set the price, invest in quality, choose advertising channels, add tables, hire people, take out a loan.

Then the market does the maths on its own. Ten thousand customers are distributed among all the players — not evenly, but depending on whose combination of price, quality, advertising and capacity looks more attractive to them. That is decided by the model, not by the facilitator.

You get your own report: revenue, costs, profit, cash on hand and market share. Then the next month begins.

After twelve months, the winner is whoever has the most cash in the register. Not whoever told the most convincing story about their strategy.

HOW THE GAME RUNS

You learn the rules as you go.
Exactly like real business.

There will be no briefing

You sit down and start playing straight away. The mechanics become clear within the first few months — just like in business, where nobody hands you a manual before opening and nobody asks whether you are allowed to make mistakes.

You make the decisions

Three to five minutes per turn. Price, quality, advertising channels and budget, tables, staff, loans. What your competitors are doing, you do not know.

The market counts

The model distributes customers among all the players and recalculates the money. No grades from the facilitator and no sympathies — only economics.

You see the result

A report on your company: revenue, costs, profit, cash, market share. Plus a shared table showing who has pulled ahead.

Next month

Strategy shows itself across a series of turns, not in a single one. A mistake in month three catches up with you in month seven, and that is the most useful thing a simulation gives you.

The final debrief

After the twelfth month we go through who did what and why it turned out this way. This is where most of the learning happens.

THE MAIN THING ABOUT THE GAME

The food court is just a shell

In the game you run a restaurant in a food court. That does not mean the game is about restaurants.

Replace the set lunch with a subscription, window fitting, a haircut, legal services, an English course or a shipment of goods from China — the model does not change by a single step. Limited demand. Competitors fighting for the same people. Price against quality. Advertising that may never pay off. Capacity that runs into money. And a cash gap that arrives before the profit does.

The food court was chosen because anyone can understand its rules in two minutes, with no industry context. After that, your own business begins — just without the risk of losing real money on it.

The same mechanics fit any business: only the sign above the door changes.

WHY PEOPLE PLAY IT

Four instruments in one format

TRAINING

Skills are built through action. Not lecture notes on unit economics, but a decision that turned into a loss a month later.

ASSESSMENT

One game shows how a person does the maths, decides on incomplete data and takes a hit. A CV shows none of that.

DIAGNOSTICS

Managerial habits show up in the very first turns. You will see your own strengths and weak spots yourself, without anyone else's grades.

PRACTICE

The game can be repeated: change strategy, try it differently and compare the result on the same model.

What one game reveals about a person

  • Whether they run the numbers or go by feel
  • What they do after the first loss
  • Whether they panic-discount when they start losing customers
  • Whether they take a loan they could do without
  • Whether they notice that advertising is not paying off
  • Whether they can stop and recalculate
  • How they react when a competitor overtakes them
  • Whether they will change strategy or cling to the first one
