WHAT IT IS

How the business game works

Several companies play on one market. The customers are shared, and every player has to pull them their way. Each turn is a game month: you set the price, invest in quality, choose advertising channels, add tables, hire people, take loans. Whether a customer walks in or goes to a competitor depends on what you decided. Exactly like a live market — and who pulled whom is worked out by the model, not by the host.

Every month there is a financial report. You see the result of your own moves and, more importantly, of your competitors': revenue, costs, profit, the going price on the market, cash in the register and market share. Then the debrief, a new strategy and the next month.

The winner is whoever ends up with the most cash in the register.

WHAT HAPPENS AT THE TABLE / ON SCREEN

You learn the rules as you go.
Exactly like real business.

There will be no briefing

You sit down and start playing straight away. The mechanics become clear within the first few months — just like in business, where nobody hands you a manual before opening and nobody asks whether you are allowed to make mistakes.

You make the decisions

Three to five minutes per turn. Price, quality, advertising channels and budget, tables, staff, loans. What your competitors are doing, you do not know.

The market counts

The model distributes customers among all the players and recalculates the money. No grades from the host and no favourites — only economics.

You see the result

A report on your company: revenue, costs, profit, cash, market share. Plus a shared table showing who has pulled ahead.

Next month

Strategy shows itself across a series of turns, not in a single one. A mistake in month three catches up with you in month seven, and that is the most useful thing a simulation gives you.

The final debrief

After the last month we go through who did what and why it turned out this way. This is where most of the learning happens.

THE MAIN THING ABOUT THE GAME

The food court is just a shell

In the game you run a restaurant in a food court. That does not mean the game is about restaurants.

Replace the set lunch with a subscription, window fitting, a haircut, legal services, an English course or a shipment of goods from China — the model does not change by a single step. Limited demand. Competitors fighting for the same people. Price against quality. Advertising that may never pay off. Capacity that runs into money. And a cash gap that arrives before the profit does.

The food court was chosen because anyone can understand its rules in two minutes, with no industry context. After that, your own business begins — just without the risk of losing real money on it.

The same mechanics fit any business: only the sign above the door changes.

AND WHY NOT JUST ASK AN AI

You are playing against people, not against models

Ask an AI for a strategy and you get a competent, lawful and completely standard answer. The trouble is that on a real market you are not up against a model — you are up against the person at the next table.

It is like chess. A game against an engine and a game against a human being are two different games. The engine looks for the best move. A person digs in, bluffs, cuts prices out of spite, copies you two months too late, or suddenly does something no calculation would ever propose. That is precisely what you cannot prepare for from a textbook.

Which is why the best table to sit at is one where the others are more experienced than you. In a single evening you see moves that appear in no manual, and you learn how people actually behave on a market.

THE MISTAKE THIS GAME EXPOSES

You can build a flawless company. The price is calculated, the quality is honest, the advertising is in the right channels, the room is sized to demand — everything adds up on paper. And you still lose, because the competitor beside you behaved in a way your model never allowed for.

What decides the fate of a business is not how the company is arranged inside, but what happens between you and the next table. No consultant will show you that — human or artificial.

FREQUENTLY ASKED

The short answers

I want to start a business but have no experience. Where do I start?

With a cheap mistake. In one evening the game walks you through what normally takes a year: you set a price, pour money into advertising, and run into a cash gap. Here the mistake costs points instead of savings. So when the same thing happens to your own money, you have already been through it and know how it ends.

How much does it cost?

Three leagues: $25 per team if you have no business yet; $100 if your business already runs — up to ten employees and up to a million dollars a year; $3000 for a strategy session for medium and large companies. The price always covers the whole team, and a team can be any size.

What is the difference between the leagues?

Two things only: the length of the game and who else is at the table. League 12 is twelve turns among other beginners. League 24 is twenty-four turns among working founders. League 36 is thirty-six turns among owners of medium and large businesses. The market model itself is the same in every league — we do not tune it to your industry.

12, 24, 36 months — how much of my time is that?

The months are game months, not calendar ones: you are not waiting a year, let alone three. The whole game runs in a single session, and one game month is one turn of three to five minutes. What differs between leagues is how many of those turns you get, and how far ahead the consequences of your decisions become visible.

Why play at all, if I can just ask an AI for a strategy?

An AI will hand you a competent, standard strategy — which is exactly what never happens on a real market. There you are up against people: they dig in, bluff, cut prices out of spite and make moves no calculation would allow for. The game gives you what no consultant can — how competitors actually behave, rather than a textbook derivation.

Is it online, or do I have to travel somewhere?

Both work. The mechanics are identical: you make decisions, the model does the maths, everyone sees the result. Offline adds live negotiation at the next table; online lets you assemble a team from different cities.

Do I need to prepare or learn the rules in advance?

No. There is no briefing before the game, and that is deliberate. The mechanics become clear within the first few months, exactly as in business, where nobody hands you a manual before opening day.

How is this different from a business course?

A course explains unit economics to you. Here you set the price yourself, lose customers yourself, and read the report that shows your advertising never paid off. Nobody hands out grades: only the market counts.

I am on my own, without a team. Can I still play?

Yes. A team of one is still a team, and the price is the same. You will be playing against the other participants, not against the host.
